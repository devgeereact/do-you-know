# Maths · Ages 9–11 · Fractions and decimals
Q: What is half of 36?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 18 ±0
Why: Share 36 into 2 equal groups: 18 in each.

Q: What is a quarter of 40?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 10 ±0
Why: Share 40 into 4 equal groups: 10 in each.

Q: What is three-quarters of 20?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 15 ±0
Why: A quarter of 20 is 5, so three quarters is 15.

Q: What is one-tenth of 70?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 7 ±0
Why: Divide by 10: 70 ÷ 10 = 7.

Q: What is 0.5 + 0.25?
Slider: 0 to 1 = 0.75 ±0
Why: A half is 0.50 and a quarter is 0.25, which make 0.75.

Q: What is 1.5 + 2.7?
Slider: 0 to 10 = 4.2 ±0
Why: Add the wholes, 1 + 2 = 3, then the tenths, 0.5 + 0.7 = 1.2, to make 4.2.

Q: Which fraction is the same as 0.5?
A) 1/4
B) 1/2*
C) 1/5
Why: Half of one whole is 0.5.

Q: Match each fraction to its decimal.
Match: 1/2 = 0.5; 1/4 = 0.25; 3/4 = 0.75; 1/10 = 0.1
Why: Quarters are 0.25 each, a half is 0.5 and a tenth is 0.1.

Q: Sort the fractions by size.
Sort: Less than a half = 1/4, 1/10, 1/3; More than a half = 3/4, 2/3, 9/10
Why: A fraction is more than a half when the top is more than half the bottom.

Q: Put the fractions in order, smallest first: 3/4, 1/4, 1/2.
Order: 1/4, 1/2, 3/4
Why: A half is the same as 2/4, so in quarters the order is 1/4, 2/4, 3/4.

# Maths · Ages 9–11 · Sequences, squares and multiples
Q: What comes next? 3, 6, 12, 24, …
Slider: 0 to 100 = 48 ±0
Why: Each number is double the one before.

Q: What comes next? 100, 90, 80, 70, …
Slider: 0 to 100 = 60 ±0
Why: The numbers go down by 10 each time.

Q: What comes next? 1, 4, 9, 16, …
Slider: 0 to 50 = 25 ±0
Why: These are the square numbers: 5 × 5 = 25.

Q: What comes next? 2, 5, 8, 11, …
Slider: 0 to 50 = 14 ±0
Why: The numbers go up by 3 each time.

Q: What comes next? 1, 2, 4, 8, …
Slider: 0 to 50 = 16 ±0
Why: Each number is double the one before.

Q: What comes next? 50, 45, 40, 35, …
Slider: 0 to 100 = 30 ±0
Why: The numbers go down by 5 each time.

Q: What comes next? 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, …
Slider: 0 to 10 = 2.5 ±0
Why: The numbers go up by a half each time.

Q: Put these numbers in order, smallest first: 12, 3, 9.
Order: 3, 9, 12
Why: 3 is smallest, then 9, then 12.

Q: Sort the numbers into multiples of 3 and others.
Sort: Multiples of 3 = 6, 9, 12; Not multiples of 3 = 10, 14, 20
Why: Count in threes: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15. The others are not on the list.

Q: Sort the numbers into square and not square.
Sort: Square numbers = 4, 9, 16; Not square = 5, 10, 12
Why: 4 = 2 × 2, 9 = 3 × 3 and 16 = 4 × 4.

# Maths · Ages 9–11 · Quantities and word problems
Q: A book costs £4.50. How much do 3 books cost, in pounds?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 13.5 ±0
Why: £4.50 × 3 = £13.50.

Q: A film starts at quarter past 2 and lasts 90 minutes. How many minutes past 3 o'clock does it end?
Slider: 0 to 60 = 45 ±0 minutes
Why: 2:15 plus 90 minutes is 3:45, which is 45 minutes past 3.

Q: A rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide. What is its area?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 40 ±0 square cm
Why: Area is length × width: 8 × 5 = 40.

Q: A rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide. What is the distance all the way round it?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 26 ±0 cm
Why: Add all four sides: 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26.

Q: 24 sweets are shared equally between 6 children. How many does each child get?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 4 ±0
Why: 24 ÷ 6 = 4.

Q: How many whole weeks are there in 91 days?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 13 ±0 weeks
Why: 91 ÷ 7 = 13.

Q: A train goes 60 miles each hour. How far does it go in 3 hours?
Slider: 0 to 200 = 180 ±0 miles
Why: 60 × 3 = 180.

Q: You pay £10 for a toy that costs £6.35. How much change should you get, in pounds?
Slider: 0 to 10 = 3.65 ±0
Why: £10 − £6.35 = £3.65.

Q: It is 4 degrees outside and the temperature falls by 9 degrees. What is the new temperature?
Slider: -20 to 20 = -5 ±0 °C
Why: 4 − 9 goes below zero to −5.

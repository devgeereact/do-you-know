# Geography · Ages 6–8 · Continents and oceans
Q: How many continents do we usually count?
Slider: 0 to 10 = 7 ±0
Why: The seven continents are Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia, Europe, North America and South America.

Q: Sort the countries by their continent.
Sort: Europe = France, Italy; Africa = Kenya, Egypt; Asia = India, Japan
Why: A continent is a very large area of land. Each of these countries lies on one of them.

Q: Which is the biggest ocean?
A) the Pacific Ocean*
B) the Atlantic Ocean
C) the Arctic Ocean
Why: The Pacific is bigger than all of the world's land put together.

Q: Which continent is the coldest and covered in ice?
A) Antarctica*
B) Africa
C) Asia
Why: Antarctica is around the South Pole, so it is freezing and covered in ice.

Q: Which city is the capital of each country?
Match: France = Paris; Italy = Rome; Spain = Madrid; Egypt = Cairo; Japan = Tokyo
Why: A capital is the city where a country's government sits.

Q: Which ocean lies between Europe and North America?
A) the Atlantic Ocean*
B) the Indian Ocean
C) the Pacific Ocean
Why: Ships and planes cross the Atlantic from Europe to North America.

Q: Which is the largest hot desert in the world?
A) the Sahara*
B) the Gobi
C) the Atacama
Why: The Sahara in Africa is the biggest hot desert on Earth.

Q: Antarctica has no countries.
A) True*
B) False
Why: Antarctica belongs to no single country. Scientists from many countries work there.

Q: Put these four oceans in order, from biggest to smallest.
Order: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic
Why: The Pacific is the biggest and the Arctic is the smallest of these four.

# History · Ages 6–8 · Before and after: timelines
Q: Put these ages in order. Start with the oldest.
Order: Stone Age, Bronze Age, Iron Age
Why: People used stone tools first, then bronze, then iron.

Q: Put these groups in order of when they lived or ruled in Britain. Start with the earliest.
Order: Romans, Vikings, Normans, Tudors
Why: The Romans came in AD 43, the Vikings from AD 793, the Normans in 1066 and the Tudors ruled from 1485.

Q: Sort the things into long ago and today.
Sort: Long ago = quill pen, knight in armour, Roman soldier; Today = tablet computer, mobile phone, microwave
Why: Things from long ago are older than the things we use today.

Q: Who built the pyramids in Egypt?
A) the ancient Egyptians*
B) the Vikings
C) the Tudors
Why: The pyramids were built as tombs for pharaohs.

Q: Which came first, the Romans or the Tudors?
A) the Romans*
B) the Tudors
C) They came at the same time.
Why: The Romans came to Britain nearly 2,000 years ago. The Tudors ruled about 500 years ago.

Q: In which year did the Great Fire of London happen?
Slider: 1600 to 1700 = 1666 ±0
Why: The fire began in September 1666.

Q: Where did the Great Fire of London start?
A) Pudding Lane*
B) Baker Street
C) Oxford Street
Why: It began in a baker's shop in Pudding Lane.

Q: Dinosaurs lived long before people.
A) True*
B) False
Why: Dinosaurs lived millions of years before the first people.

Q: What is a timeline for?
A) to show events in the order they happened*
B) to count your steps
C) to measure the weather
Why: A timeline puts events in order from first to last.

# Bible & Faith · Ages 6–8 · The Christian year
Q: Put these Christian times in order, starting with Advent.
Order: Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter
Why: Advent comes before Christmas. Lent leads up to Easter.

Q: Put these in order, starting with Palm Sunday.
Order: Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Easter Sunday
Why: Palm Sunday begins Holy Week. Good Friday comes five days later and Easter Sunday ends the week.

Q: Match each Christian celebration to the Bible story it remembers.
Match: Christmas = the birth of Jesus; Good Friday = Jesus on the cross; Easter Sunday = Jesus rose again; Pentecost = the Holy Spirit came
Why: Each of these times remembers a story in the gospels or in the book of Acts.

Q: On Palm Sunday, what do Christians remember?
A) Jesus riding into Jerusalem*
B) Jesus being born
C) the Holy Spirit coming
Why: The gospels say that crowds welcomed Jesus into Jerusalem a week before Easter.

Q: What is Lent?
A) the 40 days before Easter*
B) the days after Christmas
C) a day in summer
Why: Lent is a time of preparing for Easter.

Q: What is Advent?
A) the weeks before Christmas*
B) the weeks after Easter
C) a harvest
Why: Advent is the time of waiting for Christmas.

Q: A harvest festival is a time to say thank you for food.
A) True*
B) False
Why: Many churches hold a harvest festival and share food.

Q: Sort the things into the Christmas story and the Easter story.
Sort: Christmas story = manger, shepherds, star; Easter story = cross, empty tomb, palm branches
Why: These are parts of the gospel stories about Jesus.

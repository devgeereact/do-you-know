# English · Ages 6–8 · Tricky words to spell
Q: Spell the word: what you did when you spoke: "I ___ hello".
Spell: said | extra: xk
Why: In "said", the letters "ai" make the sound "e". It is a tricky word to learn by heart.

Q: Spell the word: the word you use to give a reason.
Spell: because | extra: zq
Why: Because: b-e-c-a-u-s-e. Learn the middle "au" and the last "se".

Q: Spell the word: someone you like and play with.
Spell: friend | extra: zp
Why: There is an "end" in friend: a friend is there to the end.

Q: Spell the word: men, women and children.
Spell: people | extra: zk
Why: "Peo" sounds like "pee" here. Learn the tricky "eo" and "ple".

Q: Spell the word: it starts a polite question: "___ you like some tea?".
Spell: would | extra: xn
Why: Would, could and should all end in "ould".

Q: Spell the word: one more time.
Spell: again | extra: xk
Why: Again: a-g-a-i-n. It sounds like "agen" but is written with "ai".

Q: Spell the word: the place where children learn.
Spell: school | extra: qv
Why: In "school" the letters "ch" make a "k" sound.

Q: Spell the word: you drink it and swim in it.
Spell: water | extra: zk
Why: In "water" the "wa" sounds like "wor".

Q: Spell the word: the question word for places.
Spell: where | extra: zk
Why: Where, when, why and who all start with "wh".

Q: Spell the word: each one: "___ day".
Spell: every | extra: zq
Why: Every has three parts to write, ev-er-y, even if you say it in two.

# English · Ages 6–8 · Listen and spell: plurals and endings
Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: more than one box.
Spell: boxes | extra: qz | speak
Why: Add "es" to words that end in x: box becomes boxes.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: more than one baby.
Spell: babies | extra: qz | speak
Why: Change the y to i and add "es": baby becomes babies.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: more than one wish.
Spell: wishes | extra: qz | speak
Why: Add "es" to words that end in sh: wish becomes wishes.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: more than one leaf.
Spell: leaves | extra: qz | speak
Why: Leaf changes the f to v and adds "es": leaves.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: what you did when you leapt over a puddle: you ___.
Spell: jumped | extra: qz | speak
Why: Add "ed" for the past: jump becomes jumped.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: what a rabbit did across the field.
Spell: hopped | extra: qz | speak
Why: Double the p, then add "ed": hop becomes hopped.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: what the bus did at the lights.
Spell: stopped | extra: qz | speak
Why: Double the p, then add "ed": stop becomes stopped.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: what you are doing when you race.
Spell: running | extra: qz | speak
Why: Double the n, then add "ing": run becomes running.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: more happy than before.
Spell: happier | extra: qz | speak
Why: Change the y to i and add "er": happy becomes happier.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: in a fast way.
Spell: quickly | extra: qz | speak
Why: Add "ly" to quick: quickly.

# English · Ages 6–8 · Fill the gap: grammar
Q: Finish the sentence about yesterday’s trip.
Gap: Yesterday we ___ to the park. = went | extra: walk, going
Why: "Went" is the past of "go".

Q: Finish the sentence about birds in a tree.
Gap: There are three ___ in the tree. = birds | extra: bird, flew
Why: More than one bird is "birds".

Q: Finish the sentence about dogs in the garden.
Gap: Every day the dogs ___ in the garden. = play | extra: plays, played
Why: Every day means it happens often, and dogs is plural, so use "play". "Played" would be about the past.

Q: Finish the sentence about the children’s coats.
Gap: The children put ___ coats on. = their | extra: there, they're
Why: "Their" means belonging to them.

Q: Finish the sentence about the cold weather.
Gap: We wore warm coats because it was ___. = cold | extra: hot, sandy
Why: You wear warm coats when it is cold.

Q: Finish the sentence about a cat on a mat.
Gap: The cat ___ on the ___. = sat, mat | extra: sit, sleep
Why: The cat sat on the mat is a sentence about the past.

Q: Finish the sentence about dinner and bed.
Gap: I ___ my dinner and then I went to bed. = ate | extra: eat, eaten
Why: "Ate" is the past of "eat".

Q: Finish the sentence about two brothers.
Gap: My brother is ___ than me. = taller | extra: tall, tallest
Why: Use "er" when you compare two people.

Q: Finish the sentence about washing up.
Gap: Yesterday we ___ the dishes after tea. = washed | extra: wash, washing
Why: Yesterday means it is over, so use the past: "washed".

Q: Finish the sentence about a letter to Gran.
Gap: I ___ a letter to my gran last week. = wrote | extra: write, written
Why: "Wrote" is the past of "write".

# English · Ages 6–8 · Match, sort and order words
Q: Match each word to its opposite.
Match: hot = cold; up = down; big = small; fast = slow
Why: Opposites mean the very opposite of each other.

Q: Match each word to its plural.
Match: mouse = mice; foot = feet; child = children; tooth = teeth
Why: These plurals change the middle or the end; they do not just add s.

Q: Match each word to a word that rhymes.
Match: cat = hat; moon = spoon; night = light; boat = coat
Why: Rhyming words end with the same sound.

Q: Sort the words.
Sort: Naming words = dog, table, school; Doing words = jump, sing, swim
Why: Nouns name things; verbs say what someone does.

Q: Sort the sentences.
Sort: Statements = It is raining., I like cake., The sun is hot.; Questions = Is it raining?, Where is my hat?, Who is that?
Why: A statement tells you something. A question asks something and ends with a question mark.

Q: Sort the words by when they happened.
Sort: Today = walk, jump, play; Yesterday = walked, jumped, played
Why: Add "ed" to many verbs to show it has already happened.

Q: Build a sentence about a bird.
Order: The, bird, sang
Why: A sentence starts with a capital letter and says who did what.

Q: Build a sentence about the park.
Order: We, played, in, the, park
Why: Say it aloud: it only makes sense one way.

Q: Build a sentence about a cake.
Order: My, mum, made, a, cake
Why: The doer comes first, then the doing, then the thing.

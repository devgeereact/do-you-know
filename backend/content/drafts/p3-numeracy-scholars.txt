# Maths · Ages 15–16 · Powers and roots
Q: What is 2 to the power of 5?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 32 ±0
Why: 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.

Q: What is 3 to the power of 4?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 81 ±0
Why: 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81.

Q: What is 3 squared plus 4 squared?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 25 ±0
Why: 9 + 16 = 25.

Q: What is the square root of 169?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 13 ±0
Why: 13 × 13 = 169.

Q: What is the cube root of 64?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 4 ±0
Why: 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.

Q: What is (−2) cubed?
Slider: -20 to 20 = -8 ±0
Why: −2 × −2 = 4, and 4 × −2 = −8.

Q: What is 2 to the power of 9?
Slider: 0 to 1000 = 512 ±0
Why: Doubling nine times from 1 gives 512.

Q: What is (−3) squared?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 9 ±0
Why: A negative times a negative is positive: −3 × −3 = 9.

Q: What is 4 to the power of 0?
Slider: 0 to 10 = 1 ±0
Why: Any number to the power of 0 is 1.

Q: What is the square root of 225?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 15 ±0
Why: 15 × 15 = 225.

# Maths · Ages 15–16 · Equations
Q: Solve: 3x + 7 = 28. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 7 ±0
Why: Take 7 from both sides to get 3x = 21, then divide by 3: x = 7.

Q: Solve: 5(x − 3) = 40. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 11 ±0
Why: Divide both sides by 5 to get x − 3 = 8, so x = 11.

Q: Solve: x/3 + 4 = 10. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 18 ±0
Why: Take 4 from both sides to get x/3 = 6, then multiply by 3: x = 18.

Q: Solve: 2x − 9 = x + 5. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 14 ±0
Why: Take x from both sides to get x − 9 = 5, so x = 14.

Q: If y = 2x + 3 and y = 15, what is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 6 ±0
Why: Take 3 from 15 to get 12, then divide by 2: x = 6.

Q: Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0. What is the larger solution?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 3 ±0
Why: The equation factorises to (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so x is 2 or 3. The larger is 3.

Q: Three consecutive whole numbers add up to 48. What is the smallest?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 15 ±0
Why: The middle number is 48 ÷ 3 = 16, so they are 15, 16 and 17.

Q: The angles of a triangle are x, 2x and 3x degrees. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 30 ±0 degrees
Why: The angles add up to 180, so 6x = 180 and x = 30.

Q: Solve the pair x + y = 10 and x − y = 4. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 7 ±0
Why: Add the equations to get 2x = 14, so x = 7 and y = 3.

Q: Half of (x + 6) equals 11. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 16 ±0
Why: Double both sides to get x + 6 = 22, so x = 16.

# Maths · Ages 15–16 · Probability and statistics
Q: Events A and B are independent. A has a probability of 0.3 and B has a probability of 0.5. What is the chance, as a percentage, of both happening?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 15 ±0 %
Why: 0.3 × 0.5 = 0.15, which is 15%.

Q: A bag holds 3 red, 5 blue and 2 green counters. What is the chance of picking blue, as a percentage?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 50 ±0 %
Why: 5 of the 10 counters are blue, so 50%.

Q: You flip two fair coins. What is the chance, as a percentage, of two heads?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 25 ±0 %
Why: 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4, which is 25%.

Q: Five numbers are 12, 15, 18, 21 and 24. What is their mean?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 18 ±0
Why: The total is 90, and 90 ÷ 5 = 18.

Q: A game has 20 equally likely outcomes and 5 of them win. What is the chance of winning, as a percentage?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 25 ±0 %
Why: 5/20 = 1/4 = 25%.

Q: The chance of rain is 35%. What is the chance of no rain, as a percentage?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 65 ±0 %
Why: The chances add up to 100%, so 100 − 35 = 65.

Q: You roll a fair dice 60 times. How many sixes would you expect on average?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 10 ±0
Why: A sixth of the rolls should be sixes: 60 ÷ 6 = 10.

Q: What is the median of 4, 9, 2, 7, 12 and 5?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 6 ±0
Why: In order: 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 12. The middle two are 5 and 7, so the median is 6.

Q: The mean of four numbers is 10. Three of them are 8, 9 and 11. What is the fourth?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 12 ±0
Why: The total must be 40, and 8 + 9 + 11 = 28, so the fourth is 12.

Q: A fair spinner has 8 equal sections and 2 are green. What is the chance of green, as a percentage?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 25 ±0 %
Why: 2/8 = 1/4 = 25%.

# Maths · Ages 15–16 · Sequences, factors and number puzzles
Q: What comes next? 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, …
Slider: 0 to 50 = 13 ±0
Why: Each number is the sum of the two before it: 5 + 8 = 13.

Q: What comes next? 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, …
Slider: 0 to 100 = 42 ±0
Why: The gaps are 4, 6, 8, 10, so the next gap is 12: 30 + 12 = 42.

Q: What is the 10th term of the sequence 3n + 2?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 32 ±0
Why: Put n = 10 into 3n + 2: 30 + 2 = 32.

Q: The sequence 5, 9, 13, 17 follows 4n + 1. What is the 20th term?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 81 ±0
Why: Put n = 20 into 4n + 1: 80 + 1 = 81.

Q: How many factors does 36 have?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 9 ±0
Why: The factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 36.

Q: What is the highest common factor of 24 and 36?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 12 ±0
Why: The biggest number that divides both 24 and 36 is 12.

Q: What is the lowest common multiple of 6 and 8?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 24 ±0
Why: The multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and of 8 are 8, 16, 24. The first match is 24.

Q: Put these in order, smallest first: 0.4, 1/3, 35%, 3/8.
Order: 1/3, 35%, 3/8, 0.4
Why: As decimals they are 0.333, 0.35, 0.375 and 0.4.

Q: Sort these larger numbers into prime and not prime.
Sort: Prime = 13, 17, 23; Not prime = 27, 33, 39
Why: The primes have only two factors. 27, 33 and 39 are all multiples of 3.

Q: Match each number to its square.
Match: 4 = 16; 6 = 36; 9 = 81; 12 = 144
Why: Squaring a number multiplies it by itself.

# English · Ages 12–14 · Spelling: difficult words
Q: Spell the word: needed, you cannot do without it.
Spell: necessary | extra: qj
Why: Necessary has one collar (c) and two sleeves (ss).

Q: Spell the word: to divide into parts.
Spell: separate | extra: qj
Why: There is "a rat" in sepArate: sep-a-rate.

Q: Spell the word: without any doubt.
Spell: definitely | extra: qz
Why: Definitely has "finite" inside it: de-finite-ly.

Q: Spell the word: to make someone feel awkward.
Spell: embarrass | extra: qj
Why: Two r and two s: embarrass.

Q: Spell the word: happened.
Spell: occurred | extra: qj
Why: Double the r before "ed": occur becomes occurred. Occurred has double c and double r.

Q: Spell the word: to provide somewhere to stay.
Spell: accommodate | extra: qj
Why: Accommodate has a double c and a double m.

Q: Spell the word: the people who run a country.
Spell: government | extra: qz
Why: Govern + ment: do not lose the n in the middle.

Q: Spell the word: the world around us.
Spell: environment | extra: qz
Why: Environ + ment: keep the n before the "ment".

Q: Spell the word: a disagreement.
Spell: argument | extra: qz
Why: Argue loses its e before "ment": argument.

Q: Spell the word: the second month of the year.
Spell: February | extra: qz
Why: February has an r after the b: Feb-ru-ary.

# English · Ages 12–14 · Listen and spell: easily confused words
Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: to change or influence: "The weather can ___ our plans.".
Spell: affect | extra: qj | speak
Why: Affect is an action. Effect is a result. A comes before E, as an action comes before its result.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the result of a change: "The medicine had a good ___.".
Spell: effect | extra: qj | speak
Why: Effect is a result. An action can have an effect.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the verb, to do again and again to improve: "I must ___ my violin.".
Spell: practise | extra: qj | speak
Why: In British English the verb is "practise" with an s, and the noun is "practice" with a c.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the noun, as in "Football ___ is on Friday.".
Spell: practice | extra: qj | speak
Why: In British English the noun is "practice" with a c.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the noun, helpful words from a friend: "Can I have some ___?".
Spell: advice | extra: qj | speak
Why: Advice, the noun, has a c like "ice". Advise, the verb, has an s.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the verb, to tell someone what to do: "I ___ you to wait.".
Spell: advise | extra: qj | speak
Why: Advise, the verb, has an s. Advice, the noun, has a c.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the head of a college, or the most important.
Spell: principal | extra: qj | speak
Why: Principal ends in "pal": the principal is your pal at the top.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: a rule or belief: "It is a ___ of fair play.".
Spell: principle | extra: qj | speak
Why: Principle and rule both end in "le".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: pens, paper and envelopes.
Spell: stationery | extra: qj | speak
Why: Stationery is spelled with an "e", like envelope and pens.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: not moving.
Spell: stationary | extra: qj | speak
Why: Stationary has an "a" like "standing still".

# English · Ages 12–14 · Verbal reasoning
Q: Chapter is to book as scene is to what?
A) play*
B) actor
C) stage
Why: A chapter is one part of a book, and a scene is one part of a play.

Q: Cub is to bear as foal is to what?
A) farm
B) horse*
C) young
Why: A cub is a young bear and a foal is a young horse.

Q: Thermometer is to temperature as ruler is to what?
A) wood
B) length*
C) straight
Why: A thermometer measures temperature and a ruler measures length.

Q: Drizzle is to downpour as breeze is to what?
A) gale*
B) cloud
C) sunshine
Why: A downpour is much stronger rain than drizzle, and a gale is much stronger wind than a breeze.

Q: Which word is the odd one out?
A) sonnet
B) haiku
C) limerick
D) novel*
Why: A sonnet, a haiku and a limerick are all poems, but a novel is a long story in prose.

Q: Which word means the same as "meticulous"?
A) careless
B) thorough*
C) hasty
Why: A meticulous person is careful about every detail, so they are thorough.

Q: Match each word to one that means the same.
Match: benevolent = kind; scarce = rare; candid = frank; vital = essential
Why: These words are synonyms: they have nearly the same meaning.

Q: Match each word to its antonym.
Match: ancient = modern; generous = mean; brave = cowardly; rough = smooth
Why: These words are antonyms: they have opposite meanings.

Q: Sort the greetings and sign-offs.
Sort: Formal = Dear Sir, Kind regards, Yours faithfully; Informal = Hiya, See ya, Cheers mate
Why: A formal letter is polite and careful. A message to a friend can be chatty.

Q: Build a sentence about the library.
Order: Please, return, your, books, on, time
Why: It is a polite request: please, then the action, then the details.

# English · Ages 12–14 · Reading and inference
Q: The lighthouse keeper climbed the 200 steps each evening to light the great lamp. Ships far out at sea relied on its beam to avoid the jagged rocks below. What does the passage suggest about the keeper's job?
A) It was easy and quick
B) It was done only in summer
C) It helped keep ships safe*
Why: Ships relied on the beam to avoid the rocks, so his job kept them safe.

Q: Although the museum was free, the queue stretched around the block. Priya wondered whether the paintings could possibly be worth the wait. What does "stretched around the block" suggest about the queue?
A) It was very long*
B) It was moving quickly
C) It was made of paintings
Why: To stretch around a whole block means the queue was very long.

Q: The headteacher announced that, owing to the storm damage, the school would be closed until further notice. Why was the school closed?
A) There was a holiday
B) The teachers were ill
C) The storm had damaged it*
Why: "Owing to the storm damage" gives the reason.

Q: Our town needs a new skate park. Young people have nowhere safe to meet, and a park would keep them active. What is the writer mainly trying to do?
A) Tell a funny story
B) Persuade readers*
C) Describe a skate park
Why: The writer gives reasons to make readers agree, which is persuasion.

Q: The old oak had stood in the village for over four hundred years. Children had climbed it, couples had met beneath it, and now a storm had brought it down. What is the tone of the passage?
A) Sad and thoughtful*
B) Angry and loud
C) Silly and cheerful
Why: It remembers the tree fondly and ends with its loss.

Q: The old oak had stood in the village for over four hundred years. Children had climbed it, couples had met beneath it, and now a storm had brought it down. What can you tell about how the villagers felt about the oak?
A) They were fond of it*
B) They disliked it
C) They never noticed it
Why: Children climbed it and couples met beneath it, so it mattered to the village.

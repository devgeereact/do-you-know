# History · Ages 12–14 · The twentieth century and before
Q: Put these twentieth-century events in order. Start with the earliest.
Order: First World War begins, Wall Street Crash, Second World War begins, Moon landing
Why: They happened in 1914, 1929, 1939 and 1969.

Q: Put these events from the Middle Ages and Tudor times in order. Start with the earliest.
Order: Magna Carta, Black Death, Wars of the Roses, Spanish Armada
Why: They happened in 1215, 1348, 1455 and 1588.

Q: In which year did the First World War begin?
Slider: 1900 to 1950 = 1914 ±0
Why: Britain joined the war in August 1914.

Q: In which year did the Second World War end?
Slider: 1900 to 1999 = 1945 ±0
Why: The war in Europe ended in May 1945 and the war in Asia in September 1945.

Q: In which year did the Berlin Wall come down?
Slider: 1950 to 2000 = 1989 ±0
Why: The wall that split the city of Berlin was opened in November 1989.

Q: Sort these into ancient, medieval and modern.
Sort: Ancient = the Colosseum, the pyramids; Medieval = Magna Carta, Black Death; Modern = Moon landing, World Wide Web
Why: Ancient times end with the fall of Rome, the medieval age lasts to about 1500 and modern times follow.

Q: Which event started the First World War in 1914?
A) the murder of Archduke Franz Ferdinand*
B) the sinking of the Titanic
C) the Wall Street Crash
Why: Ferdinand was killed in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 and countries went to war within weeks.

Q: Who was the British Prime Minister for most of the Second World War?
A) Winston Churchill*
B) Neville Chamberlain
C) Clement Attlee
Why: Churchill led Britain from May 1940.

Q: Match each campaigner or leader to what they are known for.
Match: Emmeline Pankhurst = votes for women; Nelson Mandela = helped end apartheid; Martin Luther King = US civil rights; Mahatma Gandhi = India's independence
Why: Each of these people worked to change the law or the way a country was run.

# Bible & Faith · Ages 12–14 · People, gospels and the Last Week
Q: Match each person in the gospels to what the gospel says about them.
Match: Zacchaeus = climbed a sycamore tree; Peter = was a fisherman; Martha = was busy serving; Nicodemus = came to Jesus by night
Why: Luke tells of Zacchaeus and Martha, John tells of Nicodemus, and the gospels name Peter as a fisherman.

Q: Sort the books into gospels and letters.
Sort: Gospels = Mark, John; Letters = Romans, Galatians
Why: The gospels tell the life of Jesus. The letters were written to early churches.

Q: In which river did John baptise Jesus, according to the gospels?
A) the Jordan*
B) the Nile
C) the Tigris
Why: Mark, Matthew, Luke and John all link John the Baptist with the River Jordan.

Q: In the gospel accounts of the Last Supper, what did Jesus share with his disciples?
A) bread and wine*
B) water and olives
C) figs and dates
Why: Matthew, Mark and Luke say that Jesus shared bread and wine. Many Christians share bread and wine in memory of this.

Q: Which book first records the Ten Commandments?
A) Exodus*
B) Genesis
C) Acts
Why: Exodus tells how Moses received the Ten Commandments.

Q: Matthew's gospel gives a long teaching of Jesus that begins with the Beatitudes. What is it called?
A) the Sermon on the Mount*
B) the Last Supper
C) the Great Commission
Why: Matthew 5 to 7 is called the Sermon on the Mount.

Q: Put these events in the life of Jesus in the order the gospels tell them.
Order: born in Bethlehem, baptised by John, Last Supper, crucifixion
Why: The gospels tell these events in this order: his birth (Matthew and Luke), his baptism, the Last Supper and his death.

Q: Put these days of Holy Week in order, starting with Palm Sunday.
Order: Palm Sunday, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Easter Sunday
Why: Palm Sunday begins Holy Week, Maundy Thursday is the day of the Last Supper, Good Friday is the crucifixion and Easter Sunday ends the week.

Q: Maundy Thursday remembers the Last Supper.
A) True*
B) False
Why: Many Christians remember the Last Supper on Maundy Thursday.

Q: What are the first words of the Lord's Prayer in Matthew's gospel?
A) Our Father*
B) Dear Lord
C) Hail Mary
Why: Matthew 6 gives the prayer, which begins "Our Father in heaven".

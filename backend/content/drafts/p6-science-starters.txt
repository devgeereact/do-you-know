# Science · Ages 4–5 · Living or not living?
Q: Which one is alive?
A) a dog*
B) a stone
C) a spoon
Why: A dog eats, grows and moves. A stone and a spoon do not.

Q: Which of these is a living thing?
A) a chair
B) a tree*
C) a cup
Why: A tree grows and needs water. A chair and a cup never grow.

Q: Sort the things into alive and not alive.
Sort: Alive = cat, flower, bird; Not alive = rock, toy, ball
Why: Living things grow, need food or water, and have babies or seeds.

Q: Sort these into things that are alive and things that are not.
Sort: Alive = fish, grass, bee; Not alive = book, shoe, spoon
Why: A fish, grass and a bee are alive. A book, a shoe and a spoon are things we make.

Q: A plant needs water to grow.
A) True*
B) False
Why: Plants drink water through their roots.

Q: What do animals need to stay alive?
A) food and water*
B) paint and glue
C) toys and games
Why: All animals need food and water.

Q: What do plants need to grow?
A) sunlight and water*
B) toys and shoes
C) a blanket
Why: Plants use light and water to grow.

Q: A seed grows into a...
A) plant*
B) stone
C) toy
Why: A seed holds a tiny new plant.

Q: Put the life of a plant in order. First to last.
Order: seed, small plant, flower
Why: A seed grows into a small plant. Later the plant can make a flower.

# Science · Ages 4–5 · Hard, soft and bendy
Q: Which one is soft?
A) a pillow*
B) a brick
C) a metal spoon
Why: A pillow is soft. A brick and a metal spoon are hard.

Q: Which one is hard?
A) a sock
B) a teddy
C) a stone*
Why: You cannot squash a stone.

Q: Sort the things into hard and soft.
Sort: Hard = brick, spoon, coin; Soft = pillow, sock, towel
Why: Hard things keep their shape when you press them. Soft things squash.

Q: Match each material to a thing that is made from it.
Match: Glass = window; Wool = jumper; Rubber = boot; Metal = key
Why: Each material is chosen because it is good for the job.

Q: Which one will bend?
A) a rubber band*
B) a stone
C) a brick
Why: Rubber stretches and bends.

Q: A window is made from...
A) glass*
B) wool
C) a sponge
Why: Glass lets light in, so we can see through it.

Q: A sponge is soft.
A) True*
B) False
Why: You can squash a sponge in your hand.

Q: Which would keep you dry in the rain?
A) a plastic coat*
B) a paper hat
C) a sponge
Why: Plastic keeps water out. Paper and a sponge soak it up.

Q: Which one can you see through?
A) glass*
B) wood
C) a brick
Why: Light goes through glass, so it is see-through.

# English · Ages 9–11 · Spelling: prefixes and endings
Q: Spell the word: not happy.
Spell: unhappy | extra: qz
Why: The prefix "un" means not. It does not change the spelling of "happy".

Q: Spell the word: to think differently from someone.
Spell: disagree | extra: qx
Why: The prefix "dis" means the opposite. Dis + agree: keep both parts whole, so disagree has just one s in the middle.

Q: Spell the word: to write again.
Spell: rewrite | extra: qz
Why: The prefix "re" means again: re + write.

Q: Spell the word: full of care.
Spell: careful | extra: qz
Why: Care + ful: the ending "ful" has only one l.

Q: Spell the word: the feeling of being happy.
Spell: happiness | extra: qz
Why: Change the y to i, then add "ness": happiness.

Q: Spell the word: very pretty.
Spell: beautiful | extra: qz
Why: Beauti + ful: it begins "beau", then "ti", then "ful".

Q: Spell the word: pleasure from doing something.
Spell: enjoyment | extra: qz
Why: Enjoy + ment: the y stays because it follows a vowel.

Q: Spell the word: to spell a word wrongly.
Spell: misspell | extra: qx
Why: Mis + spell: there are two s letters in the middle because both parts keep theirs.

Q: Spell the word: an exciting journey or experience.
Spell: adventure | extra: qz
Why: Ad-ven-ture: the ending is "ture", not "cher".

Q: Spell the word: not the same.
Spell: different | extra: qz
Why: Different has three parts, dif-fer-ent, with a double f.

# English · Ages 9–11 · Listen and spell: words that sound alike
Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: it means belonging to them: "The children took ___ bags.".
Spell: their | extra: qz | speak
Why: "Their" shows belonging. Remember "heir" inside it: an heir owns things.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: it means in that place: "Put it over ___.".
Spell: there | extra: qz | speak
Why: "There" is a place word, like "here". Both contain "here".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: to listen with your ears.
Spell: hear | extra: qz | speak
Why: You hear with your ear: hEAR.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: in this place, not there.
Spell: here | extra: qz | speak
Why: "Here" is a place word, and it hides in "there" and "where".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: all of something, not a part.
Spell: whole | extra: qz | speak
Why: In "whole" the w is silent. A hole in the ground has no w at all.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: a gap you dig in the ground.
Spell: hole | extra: qz | speak
Why: A hole is a gap, with no "w".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: to smash something, or a rest between lessons.
Spell: break | extra: qz | speak
Why: "Break" has "ea" like "great" and "steak".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: what a cyclist squeezes to slow down.
Spell: brake | extra: qz | speak
Why: Brake has an "a_e" pattern: the vowel sound comes from the silent e.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the past of know.
Spell: knew | extra: qz | speak
Why: Knew starts with a silent k, like "know".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: not old.
Spell: new | extra: qz | speak
Why: New has no silent k. It sounds the same as knew.

# English · Ages 9–11 · Grammar and punctuation
Q: Which sentence uses commas in a list correctly?
A) We bought apples, pears, plums and grapes.*
B) We bought, apples pears plums, and grapes.
C) We bought apples pears, plums and, grapes.
Why: Commas separate the items in a list, and "and" takes the place of the last comma.

Q: Which sentence puts the comma in the right place after the opening phrase?
A) After the storm, we cleaned the garden.*
B) After the storm we, cleaned the garden.
C) After, the storm we cleaned the garden.
Why: A comma comes after a phrase that opens the sentence, such as "after the storm".

Q: Which word is the adverb in: "The tortoise walked slowly."?
A) tortoise
B) walked
C) slowly*
Why: An adverb tells you how something is done. "Slowly" tells you how it walked.

Q: Choose the joining word for the umbrella sentence.
Gap: I took an umbrella ___ it was raining. = because | extra: but, or
Why: "Because" gives the reason for taking the umbrella.

Q: Choose the joining word for the outside sentence.
Gap: We wanted to go outside, ___ it was too wet. = but | extra: so, or
Why: "But" joins two ideas that pull against each other.

Q: Sort the words by what they do in a sentence.
Sort: Nouns = river, teacher; Adjectives = tiny, golden; Verbs = devour, gather
Why: Nouns name things, adjectives describe them and verbs say what happens.

Q: Which word is the pronoun in: "She picked up the book."?
A) She*
B) picked
C) book
Why: A pronoun stands in for a name. "She" stands in for a girl or woman.

Q: One dog owns a bone. Which sentence is correct?
A) The dogs bone was buried.
B) The dog's bone was buried.*
C) The dogs' bone's was buried.
Why: An apostrophe before the s shows that one dog owns the bone.

Q: Match each word to its meaning.
Match: unkind = not kind; redo = do again; misread = read wrongly; disagree = not agree
Why: A prefix at the start changes the meaning of the word.

Q: Build a sentence about the weather.
Order: It, was, a, windy, day
Why: A sentence needs a subject, a verb and a full stop.

# English · Ages 9–11 · Reading short passages
Q: Maya's grandfather kept bees at the bottom of his garden. Every summer she helped him collect the honey. She wore a net hat so the bees could not sting her face. Why did Maya wear a net hat?
A) To stop the bees stinging her face*
B) To keep her hair tidy
C) Because it was raining
Why: The passage says the hat stopped the bees stinging her face.

Q: The Thames is the longest river that flows entirely in England. It flows through London and into the North Sea. Many bridges cross it, including Tower Bridge. Which sea does the Thames flow into?
A) The Irish Sea
B) The North Sea*
C) The Black Sea
Why: The second sentence says it flows into the North Sea.

Q: Tom had trained for weeks. On race day his legs felt heavy and his heart pounded, but he kept going. When he crossed the line he was smiling. How did Tom feel when the race ended?
A) Scared
B) Bored
C) Pleased*
Why: He was smiling as he crossed the line, so he felt pleased.

Q: The sky turned dark and the wind began to howl. Sam grabbed his coat and ran for home. What was the weather probably about to do?
A) Turn into a storm*
B) Become a calm, hot day
C) Snow all night
Why: A dark sky and a howling wind are signs of a storm.

Q: The shy girl hid behind her mum and would not speak to anyone. What does the word "shy" mean here?
A) Very tall
B) Nervous with other people*
C) Angry
Why: She hid and would not speak, so she felt nervous.

Q: Moths fly at night, while butterflies fly by day. Butterflies fold their wings upright when they rest, but moths usually spread them flat. How is a resting butterfly different from a moth?
A) It folds its wings upright*
B) It spreads its wings flat
C) It cannot fly
Why: The passage says butterflies fold their wings upright when they rest.

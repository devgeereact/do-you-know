# Maths · Ages 12–14 · Percentages and ratio
Q: What is 12% of 250?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 30 ±0
Why: 10% of 250 is 25 and 1% is 2.5, so 12% is 25 + 5 = 30.

Q: What is 25% of 60?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 15 ±0
Why: 25% is a quarter: 60 ÷ 4 = 15.

Q: What is 15% of 200?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 30 ±0
Why: 10% is 20 and 5% is 10, so 15% is 30.

Q: A coat costs £40 and is reduced by 20%. What is the sale price, in pounds?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 32 ±0
Why: 20% of £40 is £8, so the price is £40 − £8 = £32.

Q: Share 20 sweets in the ratio 1 : 3. How many are in the larger share?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 15 ±0
Why: 1 + 3 = 4 parts, so one part is 5. The larger share is 3 × 5 = 15.

Q: A recipe uses 2 cups of flour with 3 cups of milk. How many cups of milk go with 6 cups of flour?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 9 ±0 cups
Why: The flour is multiplied by 3, so the milk is too: 3 × 3 = 9.

Q: What is 3/5 as a percentage?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 60 ±0 %
Why: 1/5 is 20%, so 3/5 is 60%.

Q: Increase 80 by 25%. What is the new amount?
Slider: 0 to 200 = 100 ±0
Why: 25% of 80 is 20, so 80 + 20 = 100.

Q: A phone costs £250 and the price rises by 10%. What is the new price, in pounds?
Slider: 0 to 500 = 275 ±0
Why: 10% of £250 is £25, so the new price is £275.

Q: What is 5% of 360?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 18 ±0
Why: 10% is 36, so 5% is half of that: 18.

# Maths · Ages 12–14 · Algebra: find the missing number
Q: Solve: 7x − 4 = 3x + 20. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 6 ±0
Why: Take 3x from both sides to get 4x − 4 = 20, add 4 to get 4x = 24, then divide by 4: x = 6.

Q: Solve: x + 9 = 25. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 16 ±0
Why: Take 9 from both sides: x = 16.

Q: Solve: 2x − 5 = 11. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 8 ±0
Why: Add 5 to both sides to get 2x = 16, then divide by 2: x = 8.

Q: Solve: x ÷ 4 = 6. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 24 ±0
Why: Multiply both sides by 4: x = 24.

Q: Solve: 5x + 2 = 37. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 7 ±0
Why: Take 2 from both sides to get 5x = 35, then divide by 5: x = 7.

Q: If y = 3x + 1 and x = 4, what is y?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 13 ±0
Why: Replace x with 4: 3 × 4 + 1 = 13.

Q: The distance round a square is 36 cm. How long is one side?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 9 ±0 cm
Why: A square has four equal sides: 36 ÷ 4 = 9.

Q: Solve: 4(x − 2) = 20. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 7 ±0
Why: Divide both sides by 4 to get x − 2 = 5, so x = 7.

Q: Solve: 2x + 3x = 45. What is x?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 9 ±0
Why: 2x + 3x = 5x, so 5x = 45 and x = 9.

Q: A number is doubled and then 7 is added to make 31. What is the number?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 12 ±0
Why: Work backwards: 31 − 7 = 24, then 24 ÷ 2 = 12.

# Maths · Ages 12–14 · Averages and reading data
Q: Five test scores are 6, 8, 7, 9 and 10. What is the mean?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 8 ±0
Why: Add the scores to get 40, then divide by 5: 8.

Q: What is the median of 8, 2, 6, 4 and 10?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 6 ±0
Why: In order the numbers are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. The middle one is 6.

Q: What is the mode of 2, 3, 3, 5, 3 and 6?
Slider: 0 to 20 = 3 ±0
Why: The mode is the number that appears most often: 3 appears three times.

Q: What is the range of 12, 7, 19 and 5?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 14 ±0
Why: Range = biggest − smallest = 19 − 5 = 14.

Q: A bar chart shows 12 cats, 18 dogs and 6 fish. How many pets are shown in total?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 36 ±0
Why: 12 + 18 + 6 = 36.

Q: A bar chart shows 12 cats, 18 dogs and 6 fish. How many more dogs than fish are there?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 12 ±0
Why: 18 − 6 = 12.

Q: Four days of ticket sales were 15, 20, 10 and 15. What is the mean?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 15 ±0
Why: The total is 60, and 60 ÷ 4 = 15.

Q: A quarter of 80 pupils walk to school. How many walk?
Slider: 0 to 100 = 20 ±0
Why: 80 ÷ 4 = 20.

Q: In a survey of 50 pupils, 40% chose pizza. How many chose pizza?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 20 ±0
Why: 10% of 50 is 5, so 40% is 20.

Q: Four temperatures were 12, 15, 11 and 14 degrees. What is the mean?
Slider: 0 to 50 = 13 ±0 °C
Why: The total is 52, and 52 ÷ 4 = 13.

# Maths · Ages 12–14 · Order and sort: negatives, fractions, primes
Q: Put these numbers in order, smallest first: −3, 5, −8, 0.
Order: −8, −3, 0, 5
Why: Negative numbers are smaller than zero, and the further from zero the smaller they are.

Q: Put these decimals in order, smallest first: 0.5, 0.35, 0.6, 0.05.
Order: 0.05, 0.35, 0.5, 0.6
Why: Compare the tenths first: 0.05 has none, 0.35 has three, 0.5 has five and 0.6 has six.

Q: Put these fractions in order, smallest first: 1/2, 1/3, 3/4, 1/4.
Order: 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 3/4
Why: With the same top number, the bigger the bottom, the smaller the fraction. 3/4 is biggest because it is more than a half.

Q: Sort the numbers into positive and negative.
Sort: Positive = 6, 3, 9; Negative = −4, −1, −7
Why: Numbers below zero have a minus sign.

Q: Sort the numbers into prime and not prime.
Sort: Prime = 2, 7, 11; Not prime = 9, 15, 21
Why: A prime number has exactly two factors, 1 and itself. 9 = 3 × 3, 15 = 3 × 5 and 21 = 3 × 7.

Q: Sort the numbers into factors of 12 and others.
Sort: Factors of 12 = 2, 3, 4, 6; Not factors = 5, 7
Why: A factor divides exactly: 12 ÷ 5 and 12 ÷ 7 leave a remainder.

Q: Match each fraction to its percentage.
Match: 1/2 = 50%; 1/4 = 25%; 3/10 = 30%; 4/5 = 80%
Why: 1/4 is 25%, 1/2 is 50%, 3/10 is 30% and 4/5 is 80%.

Q: Match each square to its answer.
Match: 3² = 9; 5² = 25; 7² = 49; 10² = 100
Why: Squaring a number multiplies it by itself.

Q: Which of these decimals is the largest?
A) 0.7*
B) 0.07
C) 0.69
Why: 0.7 is the same as 0.70, which is bigger than 0.69 and 0.07.

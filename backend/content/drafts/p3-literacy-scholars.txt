# English · Ages 15–16 · Spelling: words from the exam list
Q: Spell the word: a regular beat in music.
Spell: rhythm | extra: qz
Why: Rhythm has no vowels except y: r-h-y-t-h-m. Rhythm helps your two hips move.

Q: Spell the word: a system with people or things ranked in levels.
Spell: hierarchy | extra: qz
Why: Hierarchy starts "hier", then "arch": hier-arch-y.

Q: Spell the word: to make something seem bigger than it is.
Spell: exaggerate | extra: qz
Why: Exaggerate has a double g, then "erate".

Q: Spell the word: the group that makes the laws.
Spell: parliament | extra: qz
Why: There is an "i" before the "a": parl-i-a-ment.

Q: Spell the word: your inner sense of right and wrong.
Spell: conscience | extra: qz
Why: Conscience contains "science": con-science.

Q: Spell the word: a dark outline against a light background.
Spell: silhouette | extra: qz
Why: Silhouette comes from French: sil-hou-ette.

Q: Spell the word: a system of rules and offices.
Spell: bureaucracy | extra: qz
Why: Bureaucracy ends "cracy" like democracy.

Q: Spell the word: keeping something in good working order.
Spell: maintenance | extra: qz
Why: Maintenance is made from "maintain" but changes the ai to ena: maintenance.

Q: Spell the word: playful in a naughty way.
Spell: mischievous | extra: qz
Why: Mischievous has three syllables, mis-chie-vous, not mischievious.

Q: Spell the word: a period of a thousand years.
Spell: millennium | extra: qz
Why: Millennium has a double l and a double n.

# English · Ages 15–16 · Listen and spell: words people get wrong
Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: a line of people waiting their turn.
Spell: queue | extra: qj | speak
Why: Queue sounds like the letter Q, followed by the silent letters "ueue".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the paper you get to prove you paid.
Spell: receipt | extra: qj | speak
Why: Receipt has a silent p: re-ceipt, and "i before e" does not apply after c.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: a special advantage that not everyone has.
Spell: privilege | extra: qj | speak
Why: Privilege: priv-i-lege. There is no d, so it does not end like "ledge".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: the one after the eleventh.
Spell: twelfth | extra: qj | speak
Why: Twelve changes its v to f before th: twelfth.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: a small group chosen to make decisions.
Spell: committee | extra: qj | speak
Why: Committee has a double m, a double t and a double e.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: a promise that something will be as described.
Spell: guarantee | extra: qj | speak
Why: Guarantee starts with "gu", like "guard".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: how hot or cold something is.
Spell: temperature | extra: qj | speak
Why: Temperature has "era" in the middle: temp-era-ture.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: something like a carrot or a cabbage.
Spell: vegetable | extra: qj | speak
Why: Vegetable: veg-e-ta-ble. Say all four parts so you do not skip the middle e.

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: a place where you pay to eat a meal.
Spell: restaurant | extra: qj | speak
Why: Restaurant ends in "aurant", with "au" as in "sauce".

Q: Listen, then spell the word. Clue: honestly and truly, as you sign a letter.
Spell: sincerely | extra: qj | speak
Why: Sincere + ly: keep the e from sincere.

# English · Ages 15–16 · Language and reading skills
Q: Which technique is used in: "The wind sang through the branches."?
A) Simile
B) Personification*
C) Alliteration
Why: The wind is given a human action, singing. That is personification.

Q: Which phrase is an oxymoron?
A) bitter cold
B) deafening silence*
C) quiet night
Why: An oxymoron joins two opposites: deafening is loud and silence is quiet.

Q: Which sentence uses sibilance?
A) The snake slid silently through the soft sand.*
B) The big black bear bounced.
C) She walked home.
Why: Sibilance repeats soft s sounds: snake, slid, silently, soft, sand.

Q: What is the effect of a very short sentence after several long ones?
A) It creates emphasis and tension*
B) It makes the text longer
C) It shows the writer ran out of ideas
Why: A short sentence stands out, so it draws attention to the point.

Q: Which word means the same as "ambiguous"?
A) Unclear*
B) Honest
C) Gigantic
Why: Something ambiguous has more than one possible meaning, so it is unclear.

Q: Match each term to its meaning.
Match: pathetic fallacy = weather mirrors mood; oxymoron = two opposites joined; juxtaposition = contrasts side by side; sibilance = repeated s sounds
Why: These are techniques that writers use to build mood and meaning.

Q: Sort the techniques by what they work on.
Sort: Sound = sibilance, alliteration, onomatopoeia; Imagery = simile, metaphor, personification
Why: Sound devices work on how the words sound. Imagery builds a picture in the reader's mind.

Q: Nobody noticed the quiet boy at the back of the room, which was exactly how he liked it. But when the teacher read out the winning poem, the whole class turned to stare at him. What does the ending suggest?
A) He had written the winning poem*
B) He was in trouble
C) He was about to leave
Why: The class turned to stare at him when the poem was read, so it was his.

Q: Plastic bottles are cheap, but they take hundreds of years to break down. If we recycled more of them, far fewer would end up in the sea. What is the writer's main point?
A) Plastic bottles are expensive
B) Recycling would reduce plastic in the sea*
C) The sea is clean
Why: The last sentence gives the main point: more recycling means less plastic in the sea.

Q: "I'm perfectly fine," said Dan, gripping the arm of his chair until his knuckles turned white. What does the writer want you to understand?
A) Dan is really calm
B) Dan is not as relaxed as he says*
C) Dan is angry with his chair
Why: His white knuckles show that his words and his feelings do not match.

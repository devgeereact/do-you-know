# Science · Ages 6–8 · Materials and what they are for
Q: Match each material to something made from it.
Match: Wood = pencil; Glass = window; Wool = jumper; Rubber = tyre; Metal = saucepan
Why: We pick a material for what it is like: strong, clear, warm, bouncy or good at heating.

Q: Sort the materials into natural and made by people.
Sort: Natural = wool, wood, stone; Made by people = plastic, glass, brick
Why: Wool, wood and stone come from sheep, trees and the ground. Plastic, glass and bricks are made in factories.

Q: Which material keeps water out?
A) paper
B) plastic*
C) cotton wool
Why: Plastic is waterproof. Paper and cotton wool soak water up.

Q: Which material is best for a window?
A) glass*
B) wood
C) wool
Why: Glass is clear, so light comes through.

Q: Why is a saucepan made of metal?
A) Metal is strong and heats up well.*
B) Metal is soft and fluffy.
C) Metal is see-through.
Why: Metal carries heat to the food and does not break when it is hot.

Q: Rubber can stretch.
A) True*
B) False
Why: A rubber band stretches and then springs back.

Q: Glass is a good material for a pillow.
A) True
B) False*
Why: Glass is hard and can break. A pillow needs to be soft.

Q: Which material comes from sheep?
A) rubber
B) wool*
C) glass
Why: Wool grows on sheep and is made into jumpers and blankets.

Q: Which word describes glass?
A) see-through*
B) stretchy
C) fluffy
Why: You can see through clear glass.

# Science · Ages 6–8 · Pushes, pulls and magnets
Q: Sort the actions into pushes and pulls.
Sort: Push = kick a ball, press a bell, push a swing; Pull = open a drawer, tug a rope, pull a trolley
Why: A push moves something away from you. A pull brings it towards you.

Q: What can a magnet pick up?
A) a steel paper clip*
B) a wooden spoon
C) a plastic cup
Why: Magnets pull on things made from iron or steel.

Q: Sort the things into those a magnet sticks to and those it does not.
Sort: Sticks to a magnet = nail, paper clip; Does not stick = wooden spoon, plastic cup, glass jar
Why: Iron and steel are magnetic. Wood, plastic and glass are not.

Q: What is a force?
A) a push or a pull*
B) a kind of food
C) a colour
Why: A force makes things start moving, stop or change direction. It is always a push or a pull.

Q: The ends of a magnet are called...
A) legs
B) poles*
C) wings
Why: Every magnet has a north pole and a south pole.

Q: Two north poles push each other away.
A) True*
B) False
Why: Poles that are the same push apart. Poles that are different pull together.

Q: A magnet can pick up a wooden spoon.
A) True
B) False*
Why: Wood is not magnetic.

Q: What slows a bike down when you brake?
A) friction*
B) a magnet
C) light
Why: Friction is a force that slows things when they rub together.

Q: Match each force to what it does.
Match: Gravity = pulls things down; Friction = slows things; Magnetism = pulls iron; Push = moves away
Why: These are four forces and what each one does.

# Science · Ages 6–8 · Food chains and habitats
Q: Put the food chain in order. Start with the plant.
Order: grass, rabbit, fox
Why: The rabbit eats the grass and the fox eats the rabbit.

Q: Put this food chain in order. Start with the leaf.
Order: leaf, caterpillar, bird
Why: The caterpillar eats the leaf and the bird eats the caterpillar.

Q: Match each animal to its home.
Match: Bird = nest; Bee = hive; Rabbit = burrow; Spider = web; Fish = water
Why: Each animal makes or finds a home that suits it.

Q: Sort the animals by what they eat.
Sort: Plant eaters = cow, rabbit, sheep; Meat eaters = lion, shark, owl
Why: Cows, rabbits and sheep eat plants. Lions, sharks and owls eat other animals.

Q: What do we call an animal that eats only plants?
A) herbivore*
B) carnivore
C) predator
Why: Herbivores eat plants. Carnivores eat meat.

Q: What do plants use to make their own food?
A) sunlight*
B) sweets
C) shadows
Why: Plants use light, water and air to make food.

Q: In a food chain, the arrow points to the animal that does the eating.
A) True*
B) False
Why: The arrow shows where the food goes: from the one eaten to the one eating.

Q: A habitat is...
A) the place where a plant or animal lives*
B) a kind of food
C) a toy
Why: A pond, a wood and the sea are all habitats.

Q: Which animal lives in the sea?
A) a whale*
B) a rabbit
C) a hedgehog
Why: A whale is a mammal that is adapted to live in the ocean.

Q: Which animal hunts other animals for food?
A) a lion*
B) a cow
C) a rabbit
Why: An animal that hunts others is called a predator.
